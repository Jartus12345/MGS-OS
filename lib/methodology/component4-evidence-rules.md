# MGS Evidence & Claim Rules v1.0

Every strategy generated by this system must obey the following rules. These rules apply to every analytical stage, without exception.

## RULE 1 — AMBITION IS NOT EVIDENCE
Leadership aspiration does not establish current reality.

## RULE 2 — INTERNAL PERCEPTION IS NOT EXTERNAL PERCEPTION
Employee opinion cannot independently establish market opinion.

## RULE 3 — ABSENCE OF VISIBILITY DOES NOT PROVE ABSENCE OF CAPABILITY
The Digital Credibility Scorecard (DCS) assesses presentation, not the entire internal organisation. "Not visible" must never be written or implied as "does not exist."

## RULE 4 — WEBSITE CLAIMS REMAIN CLAIMS
A public claim establishes how the organisation presents itself, not necessarily objective truth. Report self-description as self-description ("the organisation currently positions itself as...") never as established fact.

## RULE 5 — REPETITION INCREASES CONFIDENCE
Independent agreement across sources strengthens evidence. Note explicitly when a theme appears across multiple independent sources.

## RULE 6 — CONTRADICTIONS MUST BE PRESERVED
Do not eliminate inconvenient evidence simply because it creates a less tidy strategy. Never quietly resolve a material contradiction.

## RULE 7 — DO NOT INVENT CAUSATION
Do not claim one event caused another unless evidence supports that conclusion.

## RULE 8 — DO NOT INVENT CUSTOMER PERCEPTION
Unless actual customer research exists, be cautious about writing what "customers believe."

## RULE 9 — DO NOT INVENT MARKET LEADERSHIP
Words such as "leading," "best," "largest," "fastest," "first-choice," "market leader" require appropriate evidence when presented as current facts. They may be used only when explicitly framed as desired future ambition.

## RULE 10 — DIAGNOSIS AND RECOMMENDATION ARE DIFFERENT
Always preserve the chain: SOURCE EVIDENCE → FINDING → MGS INTERPRETATION → STRATEGIC IMPLICATION → RECOMMENDATION. Never collapse these stages into a single unsupported assertion.

## GOLDEN RULE
**NEVER STRENGTHEN A CLAIM BEYOND THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTING IT.**

## EVIDENCE TYPES (classify every significant piece of information)
- **FACT** — objectively established.
- **LEADERSHIP INTENT** — leadership ambition or belief.
- **INTERNAL PERCEPTION** — employee experience or belief.
- **EXTERNAL OBSERVATION** — observable external presentation.
- **MGS INTERPRETATION** — a strategic conclusion derived from evidence.

## EVIDENCE STRENGTH (classify every significant finding)
- **STRONG** — multiple independent sources and/or objective evidence.
- **MODERATE** — clearly supported by one reliable source, potentially partially corroborated.
- **EMERGING** — interesting but limited.
- **UNSUPPORTED** — claim or ambition exists but current evidence does not substantiate it. Unsupported does NOT mean false — it means MGS cannot currently present it as established truth.

## WHEN EVIDENCE IS MISSING
Do not invent an answer because the structure expects one. Where strategically important information is missing, create an **Evidence Gap** entry: what is unknown, why it matters, what would resolve it (leadership clarification, additional employee research, customer research, competitor analysis, additional documentation, market research, or MGS judgement).

## WHEN CONTRADICTIONS OR JUDGEMENT CALLS ARISE
Where evidence significantly conflicts, information is missing, a leadership ambition is unsupported, two credible strategic routes exist, a recommendation would materially reposition the business, or confidence is low — do not silently choose. Flag it as **MGS STRATEGIC JUDGEMENT REQUIRED** with: the issue, the relevant evidence, plausible interpretations, and the decision MGS needs to make.
