# MGS Writing Standard v1.0

The completed strategy must sound like an experienced human strategist has spent considerable time understanding the organisation. It must not sound like generic AI consultancy.

- WRITE CONCLUSIONS, NOT MOTIVATIONAL LANGUAGE. Avoid "This creates an exciting opportunity to unlock significant potential." Prefer "The opportunity is to make more of the expertise that already exists within the organisation."
- AVOID ARTIFICIAL DRAMA. Do not repeatedly write "The problem is...", "The reality is...", "This matters because...", "The answer is...", "This is critical...". Use developed professional reasoning.
- AVOID REPETITIVE AI RHETORIC. Do not repeatedly write "This isn't about X. It's about Y.", "Not X. Not Y. But Z.", "The opportunity isn't X. It is Y." Occasional use may be natural; repeated use is not.
- REMOVE CONSULTANCY FILLER: unlock potential, elevate, leverage synergies, drive meaningful engagement, dynamic landscape, ever-evolving landscape, authentic storytelling, meaningful connections, cut through the noise, transformative journey. Use straightforward professional language.
- USE CLIENT LANGUAGE WHERE USEFUL. If leadership or employees naturally use a phrase that accurately captures something important, retain it rather than translating everything into formal consultancy terminology.
- MATCH CONFIDENCE TO EVIDENCE. Use "suggests," "indicates," "appears," "leadership identified," "employees consistently described," "our external assessment found" where qualification is required. Do not weaken genuinely strong evidence unnecessarily either.
- DO NOT INSULT THE CLIENT. Identify weaknesses professionally. Avoid "The existing brand is poor and outdated." Prefer "The existing identity provides a credible foundation, although aspects no longer fully support the direction leadership wants to pursue."
- USE PROFESSIONAL PARAGRAPHS. Avoid an excessive number of one-line statements. Use developed paragraphs where reasoning needs explanation; use bullets only where they materially improve clarity.
- AVOID GENERIC STRATEGY LANGUAGE. Every important paragraph should feel traceable back to the client's evidence. The strategy should not sound like it could be sent to another organisation after replacing the company name.
