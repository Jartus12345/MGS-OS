# MGS Cross-Analysis Methodology v1.0

Purpose: understand what becomes visible when the organisation's current public position, leadership ambition, employee reality and external presentation are compared against one another. This is not a combined summary. Follow the stages in order.

## STAGE 1 — THEME EXTRACTION
Identify important themes naturally occurring across the evidence. Do not begin with predetermined marketing categories (examples only, not a checklist: trust, culture, expertise, heritage, innovation, specialisation, service, relationships, recruitment, geography, leadership, technology, premium positioning). The evidence must determine the themes. Build a matrix: Theme × {Company Context, Leadership, Employees, External}.

## STAGE 2 — AGREEMENTS
Identify where two or more independent sources support materially the same conclusion. Agreements may reveal genuine strengths, cultural characteristics, credible differentiators, existing brand equity, consistency, credible foundations for positioning. Always ask: is this merely true, or is it strategically useful? (e.g. professionalism may be true but generic.)

## STAGE 3 — CONTRADICTIONS
Identify where sources materially disagree. For every material contradiction establish: what each source says; why the disagreement matters; whether other evidence resolves it; if unresolved, what MGS needs to clarify; whether it should become a client alignment question. Do not quietly resolve contradictions — preserve them.

## STAGE 4 — PERCEPTION GAPS
A Perception Gap exists where something appears to exist internally or is strategically important but is not adequately represented externally. Possible forms (do not force these — discover from evidence): Internal Strength → Low External Visibility; Desired Future Position → Current Position; Business Evolution → Historical Brand Perception; Strong Culture → Weak Employer Brand Visibility; Expert People → Faceless External Communication; Strategic Capability → Service-Led Positioning. For each gap explain: what exists or is desired; what is currently visible; evidence supporting both sides; why the gap matters; strategic implication.

## STAGE 5 — HIDDEN STRENGTHS
Identify strengths that repeatedly appear through research but are not currently being strategically exploited. Flag as POTENTIAL HIDDEN STRENGTH. Do not automatically put them into the strategy — test commercial relevance first.

## STAGE 6 — UNSUPPORTED AMBITIONS
Leadership may want the organisation to become known for something the current evidence does not yet support. Do not reject the ambition automatically. Distinguish "existing strength" from "desired future association." Example: leadership wants innovation leadership but other evidence does not support strong existing innovation authority → correct interpretation: "Innovation leadership is currently a desired future association rather than an established market position." Strategic implication should typically be build evidence → demonstrate → earn authority, rather than simply communicate more loudly.

## STAGE 7 — UNDERUSED EVIDENCE
Identify useful substance that already exists but receives limited external visibility (case studies, client relationships, outcomes, expert people, research, awards, certifications, proprietary technology, long-term relationships, evidence of delivery). Sometimes the answer is not "create something new" but "use the proof that already exists."

## STAGE 8 — CURRENT vs DESIRED ANALYSIS
For every significant strategic theme establish: CURRENT (what evidence suggests the organisation currently communicates/represents); DESIRED (where leadership wants this to move); SUBSTANCE (what evidence currently exists to support the desired position); GAP (what prevents the organisation from credibly occupying that position today); STRATEGIC IMPLICATION (what needs to happen).

## STAGE 9 — STRATEGIC SIGNIFICANCE
Not every insight belongs in the strategy. Every potential finding must pass: EVIDENCE TEST (can it be traced to research?); SIGNIFICANCE TEST (does it materially affect commercial direction, reputation or desired perception?); MGS INFLUENCE TEST (can brand, marketing or communications meaningfully influence it?). Classify each as CRITICAL / IMPORTANT / SUPPORTING / NOT STRATEGIC.

## STAGE 10 — STRATEGIC DIAGNOSIS
From the complete cross-analysis produce approximately 3–6 major strategic findings — actively resist producing 15–30 insights. The goal is the fewest findings required to accurately explain the organisation. Then produce a CENTRAL STRATEGIC DIAGNOSIS (what exists today; where leadership wants the organisation to go; what currently prevents the external position from fully supporting that ambition; the fundamental strategic opportunity) and a CENTRAL PERCEPTION MOVEMENT expressed as FROM → TO. This FROM → TO is an internal strategy mechanism, not external copy. It should describe movement without unnecessarily insulting the client's current position.
