# MGS Research Analysis Protocol v1.0

Each research source answers a different question. Understand exactly what each source can and cannot establish, and never blur these boundaries.

## INPUT 1 — COMPANY CONTEXT
Purpose: what is the organisation, and what does it currently say about itself?

CAN establish: current stated positioning; current service/product presentation; public messaging; available case studies; existing claims; factual company information where supported; what the company currently chooses to emphasise.

CANNOT establish: that its positioning is accurate; that customers perceive the organisation this way; that stated differentiators genuinely differentiate; that the most prominent website services are commercially most important; that leadership wants to retain current positioning; that self-described market leadership is objectively true.

Example — website says "We are a leading provider of X." Correct: "The organisation currently positions itself as a leading provider of X." Incorrect: "The organisation is a leading provider of X."

## INPUT 2 — STRATEGIC BUSINESS DISCOVERY (completed by senior leadership)
Purpose: where does leadership want the organisation to go?

May cover: commercial objectives; desired growth; ideal clients; priority markets/sectors; services to grow/reduce; desired reputation and perception; geographic priorities; competitive landscape; desired thought leadership; positioning; organisational challenges; future ambition; communication principles; what leadership believes differentiates the organisation.

This input provides authoritative evidence of LEADERSHIP INTENT. It does NOT automatically provide proof of external or internal reality.

AMBITION ≠ REALITY. BELIEF ≠ EVIDENCE. DESIRED PERCEPTION ≠ CURRENT PERCEPTION.

## INPUT 3 — EMPLOYEE BRAND SURVEY
Purpose: what appears to exist inside the organisation?

Covers: culture; employee experience; strengths/weaknesses; organisational values; expertise; internal reputation; pride; frustrations; change; customer relationships employees observe; differentiation; internal perception of external reputation; whether employees understand the brand and strategic direction.

Strength of a finding increases where similar themes independently appear across different individuals, departments, roles and seniority levels.

CAN establish: patterns of internal perception (e.g. "Collaboration was repeatedly identified by employees across multiple departments as a strength.")
CANNOT automatically establish: external market truth. If employees say "clients trust us," the correct framing is "employees consistently perceive client trust as an organisational strength" — then seek corroboration (repeat business, retention, testimonials, case studies, relationship length, leadership evidence).

## INPUT 4 — DIGITAL CREDIBILITY SCORECARD (DCS)
Completed independently from the client team, ideally by an assessor with no privileged understanding of the client's commercial objectives.

Purpose: if someone encountered this business externally with no internal knowledge, what could they currently understand and perceive?

Assesses: external credibility, clarity, differentiation, positioning, visual presentation, website, LinkedIn, social channels, people visibility, expertise visibility, messaging, supporting evidence, thought leadership, consistency, overall digital impression.

CAN establish: what is observable externally.
CANNOT establish: internal capability. "Specialist expertise is difficult to identify externally" is correct; "the organisation lacks specialist expertise" is not.

NOT VISIBLE ≠ DOES NOT EXIST — this must be respected permanently.

## OPTIONAL ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE
Additional evidence (brand guidelines, customer research, competitor research, commercial reports, previous strategies, internal planning documents, case studies, meeting notes, market analysis, leadership notes) may be supplied. For each, first establish: who produced it, why it was produced, and what type of evidence it can legitimately provide. Do not treat all documents as equally authoritative.

## INDEPENDENT ANALYSIS RULE
Analyse the four core inputs (plus classified additional evidence) INDEPENDENTLY before any cross-analysis occurs. Produce:
- COMPANY CONTEXT ANALYSIS — what does the organisation currently say it is?
- LEADERSHIP ANALYSIS — where does leadership want the organisation to go?
- EMPLOYEE ANALYSIS — what appears to exist internally?
- EXTERNAL ANALYSIS — what can an outsider currently see and understand?

At this stage: DO NOT reconcile sources. DO NOT develop strategy. DO NOT choose which source is correct. DO NOT develop positioning. DO NOT write a North Star. DO NOT generate recommendations. Preserve disagreement.
